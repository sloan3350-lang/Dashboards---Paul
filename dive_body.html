    The divers-down flag, which is state law and not etiquette

    Florida Statute 327.331 requires a divers-down flag or buoy prominently displayed in the area you are diving, except when you are in an area customarily used for swimming only. A flag flown from a buoy or float towed by the diver must be at least 12 by 12 inches; one flown from a vessel or structure must be at least 20 by 24 inches. The flag is red with a white diagonal stripe from the top staff-side corner to the opposite lower corner, and the stripe must be 25 percent of the flag's height.

    Vessels other than law enforcement or rescue must slow to headway speed within 100 feet of a flag on a river, inlet or navigation channel, and within 300 feet on any other water. Violation is a noncriminal infraction under s. 327.73. Statute text read directly.

    What that means at Blue Heron Bridge. You are diving inside a marked navigation channel basin with boat traffic on both sides of the span. The flag is not optional there, the 100-foot rule is the one that applies, and a towed float is the practical answer because you move a long way underwater on a single dive.

    The sanctuary, and what it forbids

    The Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary covers the whole chain. Spearfishing is prohibited within the sanctuary. Anchoring on living coral is prohibited anywhere in it. There are 17 Sanctuary Preservation Areas covering about 12 square miles, placed on the reefs people actually visit — Molasses, Key Largo Dry Rocks, Alligator, Sombrero, Looe Key and the rest. Diving and snorkelling are allowed inside an SPA; fishing and collecting are not. Anchoring inside an SPA when a mooring buoy is available is prohibited.

    The sanctuary maintains more than 600 mooring buoys, free and first-come. If you are on a charter the crew handles this; if you ever run your own boat out there, the buoy is the only legal way to sit on most of these sites.

    Do not touch the coral. Taking or damaging coral or live rock is prohibited sanctuary-wide, and that includes standing on it, resting a knee on it and steadying yourself with a hand. Buoyancy is a legal issue here, not just a skill issue.

    Spearfishing outside the sanctuary

    Spearfishing is prohibited in Monroe County from Long Key north to the Dade County line, and everywhere within 100 yards of a public swimming beach, a public fishing pier, or a bridge from which fishing is permitted. It is banned outright in all Florida state parks and in Everglades and Dry Tortugas. Between the sanctuary ban and those buffers, there is effectively nowhere on this page where you may spear.

    The licence trap

    Your free resident shoreline licence does not cover you in the water. It covers fishing from shore, a pier or a bridge you walked onto. It expressly does not cover swimming or diving, so it does not cover taking lobster on a dive, and it does not cover spearfishing. That needs the full saltwater licence, plus the lobster permit for lobster. Same trap the Surf Command Rules tab flags for the Keys bridges.

    Certification, honestly

    You are Open Water. That is the card for everything on this page down to about 60 feet, which includes every shore site, the whole first and second reef, the Benwood at 25 to 45 feet, Cannon Beach, Sombrero, Looe Key and Alligator. It is not the card for the wrecks that make the Keys famous. Operators enforce this at the dock, and they are not negotiating:

Every outfitter on the Outfitters tab teaches it. Do it in Key Largo on a trip where the training dives are on the wrecks you want to come back to, rather than in a quarry, and the course pays for itself in access.

    What is verified, what is not, and where to send more

    Same tier convention as Surf Command. Tier B means read off the operator's, park's or agency's own page. Tier C means an aggregator, a review site or a dive-media article and not confirmed at the source. Every card carries its tier. Nothing here is Tier A, because Tier A would mean I stood on the beach.

    The Drive folder is live. My Drive → Dive Command, with three subfolders: 00 — INBOX for Grok, 01 — Verified, 02 — Reference and conflicts. Anything dropped there gets read, cross-checked against what is already here, and either merged or filed as a conflict.

    How this page treats a conflict

    If two sources disagree on a fee, an hour or a depth, the card says so rather than picking one. That already happened once here: Fort Zachary Taylor's entry fee is quoted as $7 for two people in a vehicle by one source and in the standard state-park structure of $8 per vehicle by another, and the card carries both.
